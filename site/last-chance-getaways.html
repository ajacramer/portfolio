Penn State DMD 300 · Spring 2025 · Independent project

Last Chance Getaways

The brief

For an independent class project, I proposed a satirical luxury travel website promoting trips to destinations threatened by climate change. I wanted people to engage with the subject through humor and the familiar experience of planning a vacation.

What I did

I developed the concept, researched luxury travel websites, directed AI-generated imagery and copy, and designed and built the site.

The result

A live website that introduces itself as a luxury travel brand, then reveals imagined futures as visitors explore. Changing imagery, alternate itineraries, countdowns, and a destination quiz make the consequences part of planning the trip.

The idea

What if a travel agency sold the last chance to visit?

I imagined a travel brand that turns environmental loss into a selling point. The site invites people to browse beautiful destinations and plan an exclusive getaway. As they explore, the language and experiences reveal what “limited time only” could mean for those places.

The contradiction

The tools became part of the question

I used AI to produce imagery and draft satirical copy while questioning its environmental and creative costs. That tension became part of the project’s premise and my reflection on making it.

Making the travel brand believable

I studied TCS World Travel, Jacada Travel, Virtuoso, Abercrombie & Kent, and Black Tomato to understand how luxury travel websites create desire. I looked at their imagery, navigation, language, and calls to action, then used those conventions to establish a convincing setting for the satire.

  • Let the imagery lead

    Large destination images and restrained copy make the first impression feel like an invitation to travel. They also give the later image changes room to register.

  • Make the next step familiar

    Calls to action invite visitors to explore destinations, find a trip, and book. Those recognizable actions carry people into the experience as the consequences emerge.

  • Keep the choices curated

    A small selection of destinations gives each location room for its own imagery, itinerary, and story.

Establish the visual direction

I chose sand, teal, navy, and gold, paired with an elegant serif and subtle textures. The palette and typography support the calm, polished tone of the fictional brand.

Refining the plan through feedback

My professor pointed me toward immersive climate fiction, including Ghost Food and his project Planet B. He also recommended Eleventy and suggested designing the key layouts before refining the interactions in the browser. That approach let me develop the visual experience and its behavior together.

Directing the AI imagery

I started with one scene: a flooded future Venice, where submarine tours and scuba diving replace gondolas. Testing the same idea across tools helped me compare realism, composition, and control before producing the rest of the destination imagery.

  • Finding the right level of detail

    My early ChatGPT experiments often looked cartoonish or introduced details I hadn’t requested. More specific prompts, including camera and lighting direction, improved the results. Editing helped refine the composition further.

  • Learning how MidJourney responded

    MidJourney produced more realistic starting points, but elaborate prompts sometimes pushed the scene too far toward an apocalypse. After reviewing its documentation, I found shorter prompts more useful for this project. MidJourney became the source of the final imagery.

  • Finishing for the page

    I edited the images for their final placements, using expansion tools to create the space needed for large heroes and text overlays. Contrast, lighting, and composition all needed to work within the page layout.

Keeping the copy on course

AI helped draft the travel language, but the satire needed frequent editing. I developed a custom prompt, kept useful passages, and rewrote copy that became too exaggerated or lost its connection to the destination. Sometimes writing it myself was faster.

Building the
destination pages

I built the site with Eleventy, Bootstrap, and JavaScript, versioned it in GitHub, and deployed it to Netlify. Working in the browser let me refine timing, transitions, and responsive layouts as the interactions developed.

  • One structure for five destinations

    A reusable Eleventy layout supports the reef, glacier, Amazon, Antarctic, and Venice pages. It keeps navigation and page structure consistent while allowing each location its own imagery, copy, and itinerary.

  • Sources beside the countdowns

    Each destination includes the source behind its selected timeline. The countdown supports the fictional offer, while the accompanying information gives visitors context for the environmental issue.

  • A direct route to the collection

    The Destinations link takes visitors to the homepage carousel. It keeps the five trips easy to reach and supports the page’s visual approach to browsing.
