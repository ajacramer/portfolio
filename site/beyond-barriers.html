Beyond Barriers

The brief

Our team was asked to explore inclusive design. We chose to develop a fitness app concept for people with mobility disabilities, considering how they could find, follow, and control a workout.

What I did

I worked with Eve Wilson and Rebecka Espinoza throughout the project. My contributions included researching interaction design for limited mobility, analyzing fitness app reviews, and sketching navigation concepts.

The result

A clickable Figma wireframe prototype and recorded pitch showing an adaptive fitness experience. The concept combines clear navigation, accessible controls, and proposed voice commands to give users more ways to manage a workout.

The pitch and prototype

The brief

Choosing where to focus

We explored three directions: accessible navigation, speech-to-text support for deaf and ESL students, and inclusive fitness. We chose fitness because it gave us a specific experience to examine, from choosing an appropriate workout to controlling it while exercising.

The idea

Considering the whole workout

We wanted accessibility to shape the experience from the first screen. Finding a suitable class, reaching a control, or returning after a pause could each affect someone’s ability to work out independently. We considered those moments alongside the exercises themselves.

Turning research into interface decisions

I researched interaction strategies for people with mobility disabilities and translated the findings into principles for our concept. I focused on the effort required to navigate, select something, and control what happens next.

  • Make controls easier to select

    Use large, clearly labeled targets with enough space between them to reduce accidental selections.

  • Keep gestures simple

    Use straightforward tap actions and provide alternatives to interactions that require dragging, double-tapping, or multiple fingers.

  • Let people move at their own pace

    Avoid timed interactions that could expire while someone is getting into position or preparing to respond.

  • Reduce precise movement

    Use selectable options where possible so completing a task doesn’t depend on accurately moving something across the screen.

  • Make frequent actions easy to find

    Prioritize search, saved workouts, and other common tasks to reduce the effort of navigating back to them.

  • Offer another way to interact

    Explore voice input alongside touch controls so users can choose what works for their situation.

Making workout choices visible

Dropdowns raised questions about visibility, scrolling, and precise selection on a small screen. I explored large category tiles and visible filters so users could see their choices before opening a menu.

Learning from existing fitness apps

I reviewed feedback about Peloton and Apple Fitness+ across app reviews, Reddit discussions, and other reviews. I looked for recurring frustrations and connected them to possible changes in our concept. This helped identify questions to design around, while our accessibility research informed how we approached them.

Bringing the concept

We developed a clickable wireframe prototype and presented it through a recorded walkthrough. The prototype brings together workout discovery, navigation, and the proposed voice-control experience. It shows how those pieces could work together across the app.

Voice commands overlay

Why we explored
voice control

Reaching a device to pause or rewind can interrupt a workout and require someone to reposition. We explored voice commands as another way to manage the session while exercising.

Make the system’s response clear

We included microphone access throughout the concept and considered feedback for activating voice input, listening, and acknowledging a request. Users need to understand whether the system is ready and whether it heard them.

“rewind exercise”Jump 15 seconds back

Allow natural phrasing

We considered how people might speak in complete sentences or add words like “please.” The proposed interaction should accommodate those habits and make the available commands easy to discover.

What the prototype establishes

Beyond Barriers brings our research into a proposed fitness experience, with particular attention to choosing workouts and controlling them with less physical effort. The next step would be testing those interactions with people who have different mobility needs, including how well touch and voice options support their routines.
